# Data decisions

The cleaning rules and metric definitions I chose for this analysis, why, and how many rows, editors or edits each one affects. The notebooks, scripts and other documents refer to them by number.

| # | Decision | Reason | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Analyze Simple English Wikipedia (`simplewiki`) | English text, so examples are readable; small enough for a laptop (1 GB download) | Covers the whole history, 2001–2026 |
| 2 | **Bots:** an edit is a bot edit if its editor is flagged as a bot, by username or by the bot group, either at the time of the edit or today. Bot edits are excluded from the analysis | Using the flag at the time keeps the normal edits of 34 experienced editors who had a bot flag only for short automated tasks; adding today's flag catches 5 recently approved bots whose older edits have no flag | Removes 2,683,109 of 8,837,069 edits by registered accounts (30.4%), from 763 accounts. Bot edits stay in `raw_events` |
| 3 | **Logged-out editors:** anonymous (IP) editors and temporary accounts are grouped as "logged-out editors". They are left out of the newcomer analysis and kept as a separate group in community health. Edits with an unknown editor type are excluded | Anonymous editors have no ID, so they can't be followed over time. Temporary accounts (since September 2025) expire after 90 days, 68% make one edit, and nothing links them to a later registration; counting them as newcomers would inflate 2025–2026 cohorts. Logged-out editing is a fifth of all edits and a large share of what gets reverted | Newcomer analysis: 2,180,372 logged-out edits (19.8% of all edits) left out; newcomer cohorts start from the 152,625 registered accounts that ever edited. Unknown type: 6,546 edits excluded (4,549 with the editor hidden by administrators, 1,997 from 9 accounts with empty type columns) |
